RV water heater not heating: The troubleshooting guide

Most people assume a dead water heater means a dead unit. It usually doesn't. The majority of no-hot-water problems trace to one small part, a flipped switch, or a valve left in winter mode. Here is how to find yours without replacing anything you don't have to.

Last reviewed: Sep 18, 2026, against current Suburban and Atwood service guidance. We update these guides when real-world data changes.

The short version: your heater runs on two separate systems, propane and electricity, and they fail independently. Decide which one is broken first, then work the simple checks before you buy parts. A full tank, open valves, power to the right side, and a clean flame solve most cases.

Start here: Pick your symptom

No hot water at all. It could be either side. Do the tank and valve checks first, they are free.

Works on propane, not electric. The problem is almost always electric-side: the element, a switch, or 120-volt power.

Warm but never hot. Usually a bypass valve left partially open, or cold water mixing into the hot line.

The free checks first

1. Is the tank actually full?

If the rig was winterized and the drain plug or anode was left, the tank is empty. An empty tank can burn out the electric element in seconds. Open a hot faucet and confirm steady water. If it sputters and stops, the tank is empty. Never run the electric element with no water in the tank. This is the single most common winter-to-spring mistake.

2. Is the bypass still in winter mode?

If the rig was winterized, the bypass valves may still be set to route water around the heater. In that mode the heater never fills and you get cold water even though everything runs. On the common three-valve setup (two on the water lines, one on the bypass loop), normal operation is the two line valves open and the center bypass valve closed. If they are set the other way, flip them and give the heater time to fill.

3. Are the outside shower faucets off?

An outdoor shower with both faucets open can let cold water cross into the hot line and dilute the heat. Close them fully, both handles, then re-test.

4. Is there a hidden switch?

Many RV water heaters have an on/off rocker switch behind the exterior access door, on the face of the unit, separate from the indoor switch. It is easy to miss and a common reason a heater suddenly stops. Check both the indoor switch and the one behind the access door.

If it will not light on propane

Work the supply before the appliance:

  • Propane on and not empty? Open the tank valve fully. If the tank is low or just refilled, air in the line can stop ignition. A common trick: light a stove burner for a moment to bleed trapped air from the lines, then try the heater.

  • 12-volt control power? The propane side runs on 12-volt DC for the ignition board, spark, and gas valve. A blown fuse kills propane ignition even while electric still works. Check the furnace/water-heater fuse.

  • Can you hear the igniter clicking? If it clicks but no flame catches, the burner tube is likely dirty or blocked. If there is no click at all, suspect power, a fuse, or the board. If it clicks for about 6 to 8 seconds then stops, that is a safety lockout, the unit did not detect a flame and is protecting itself. Turn the heater off, then on again after a short wait to reset it.

  • Flame color tells the story. When it runs, the flame should be a steady blue. A yellow or flickering flame means incomplete combustion, usually a dirty burner or a spider nest in the tube. Cleaning the burner tube is a routine owner job.

If it will not heat on electric

The electric side is simpler:

  • 120-volt power. Check the breaker and confirm you are on shore power or generator. No 120 volts, no heat.

  • Both switches on. Indoor switch plus the rocker behind the access door, as above.

  • The heating element. If power is present and the thermostat is fine but the water stays cold, the element is the usual suspect. It can be tested with a multimeter (continuity through the element, no continuity to the tank) and replaced for a modest part cost. When one element fails, buy the same rating, do not guess wattage.

  • Gas works but electric does not: almost always the electric element or its 120-volt supply. The two systems share only the tank, so this split narrows it fast.

Overheating or lukewarm

  • Overheating or heater will not shut off: usually a thermostat stuck closed, a build-up of sediment at the bottom of the tank, or the ECO (emergency cutoff) not doing its job. Sediment is worth addressing, an anode that is fully consumed leaves the tank unprotected against corrosion.

  • Warm but not hot: bypass valve partially open, a mix valve added in line, or cold water crossing in. A single-handle faucet left in the middle can pull from both lines.

  • Strange smells or soot: usually a combustion problem, a dirty burner or a blocked vent. If you smell ammonia or see heavy soot, stop using the propane side and have it looked at.

Owner-doable vs call a tech

You can reasonably replace the heating element, thermostat, ECO, fuses, and clean the burner. You should hand off a tank leak, a failed gas valve, a bad circuit board, or anything involving the propane combustion chamber you are not comfortable with. When a repair quote approaches the price of a new unit, ask about the labor separately, RVers replace these units regularly.

My heater works on propane but not electric, what does that tell me?

It isolates the fault to the electric side. Check the breaker, both switches, the 120-volt supply, then the heating element and its wiring. The propane system and the tank are fine, which is why the two-system split is the fastest diagnostic.

How do I test the RV water heater element with a multimeter?

Disconnect power first. Set the meter to ohms. Touch the probes to the two element terminals, a healthy element reads a low resistance, a dead one reads open (infinite). Then touch one probe to a terminal and the other to the tank body, it should read open, continuity there means the element has shorted and must be replaced. Match the wattage on the replacement to the original.

Why did my heater stop making hot water after winter storage?

Most common: the drain plug or anode was left out so the tank is empty, or the bypass valves were never returned to normal operation, or the electric element burned out because the heater ran dry. Check those three before anything else.

Can I run the water heater on propane while on shore power?

Yes, most units run one heat source at a time from the selector switch, and propane is the higher-output option. Running both at once on older units can draw more current, follow your model's switch. The important rule is never run the electric element with an empty tank.
