RV black tank routine: How to dump, treat, store, and diagnose

Follow these simple steps to always maintain your RV's black tank. Only dump when the tank is nearly full, because the volume behind the waste is what carries it out. Always treat after every dump. Store the RV with the black tank drained, rinsed, and winterized. And when the tank meter is not functioning correctly, it is likely dirty, not broken: cleaning the sensors is the first thing to try.

By OriginRV. Last reviewed on Oct 14, 2026.

The short version: Let the tank fill, then dump only when it is nearly full, black first and gray second. Always treat after every dump, and give the treatment enough water to work in: three or four full bowls is about right, and Jayco's manual calls for at least two gallons. Store the RV with the black tank drained, rinsed, and winterized. And when the tank meter is not functioning correctly, it is likely dirty, not broken: cleaning the sensors is the first thing to try.

Start here: The gauge that reads full on an empty tank

This is the complaint that sends most people looking for parts, and it usually is not a broken part. Most factory tank sensors are conductivity probes mounted through the tank wall. When liquid touches a probe it completes a circuit and the panel lights that level. Waste, toilet paper fiber or soap film left on a probe completes the same circuit with the tank nearly empty, which is why a panel that reads a third or two thirds on a freshly dumped tank is almost always reporting residue rather than depth.

So the first move is a clean, not a purchase: fill the tank partway with water, add a treatment that targets paper, let it sit for the time the label gives, drive if you can so the motion works the residue loose, and dump only when the tank is nearly full. Using the black tank flush while dumping helps as well. If the reading survives that, the tank sensor guide separates a fouled sensor from a wiring fault with two tests, and covers what cleaning cannot reach.

May cause damage Never turn the flush on with the drain valve closed. The flush sprays water into the tank, and with the valve shut that water has nowhere to go. The tank fills, then overflows back through the toilet and the roof vent. A black tank emptying itself into the living space is the thing every step on this page exists to prevent. Open the drain valve before you turn the flush on. Never leave a hose connected to the flush when you are not using it.

Dump

Keep the black tank valve closed, and never leave it open. Let the tank fill to nearly full before dumping, because the volume of liquid behind the waste is what carries the solids out. Emptying a part-full tank moves the liquid and leaves the solids. Use enough water with every flush that the waste keeps moving rather than settling on the tank floor. When you do dump, it is black first and gray second, so the gray water rinses the outlet and the hose behind the black water.

The valve should always stay closed at a full hookup. When left open, the liquid drains away as you use the toilet and the solids stay behind with too little water to carry them out of the tank, which is how a tank builds the mound that later blocks it. Our black tank guide has the seven-step dump procedure, the water volumes Jayco's manual gives, information about the tank flush, and the tank heaters.

Treat

Properly treat the tank after every dump, and understand the two families of RV black tank products and their different roles as tools. Enzyme and bacteria treatments break waste and paper into smaller particles, and they need a clean tank to work: residue from a chemical treatment kills the enzymes, so a tank that has had chemicals in it wants cleaning before an enzyme product will do its job. Chemical treatments do their own work by a different route.

What never goes in is anything that does not dissolve: feminine hygiene products, paper towels and wipes, and household toilet paper that resists breaking down. Undiluted bleach, caustic drain openers and automotive antifreeze damage the seals and the tank, and any formaldehyde-based product is best left out of an RV black tank as well. Our black tank guide carries the wording straight from Jayco's manual and from Thetford.

Store

Before the first freeze, empty both waste tanks, black and gray, and pour RV antifreeze down the toilet into the black tank. Only RV antifreeze goes in. Automotive antifreeze is a different product, and it is toxic: it must never reach the fresh water system, the water heater, the water filter or the refrigerator. A coach with a macerator gets antifreeze run through the pump until it discharges at the nozzle.

While the coach is in use, keep water in the toilet bowl: that water is the vapor barrier between the tank and the living space, and a dry bowl is a straight path for tank air and hard on the seal. For winter, run the flush until antifreeze comes through into the bowl, and get enough into the tank, through the line or straight from the jug, to keep any water left in it from freezing. Leave that same antifreeze standing in the bowl the way water normally sits, deep enough that it will not evaporate away before spring: it keeps the bowl valve seal submerged so the rubber stays healthy through the winter, and it seals the tank off from the room the same way water does. In spring, flush it and put water back. The winterizing guide covers the plumbing side, and the black tank guide has the tank-specific steps, including the tank heaters and blowing out a black tank flush line.

Why does my black tank read full when it is empty?

Because factory sensors measure conductivity, not depth. Residue left on a probe completes the circuit with the tank nearly empty. Clean the tank and the probes first: a part-full fill of water with a paper-targeting treatment, time, motion if you can drive, and a dump at a nearly full tank. If the reading survives that, the sensor guide's two tests separate a fouled sensor from a wiring fault.

In what order do I dump the tanks?

Black first, then gray. The gray water follows the black through the same hose and rinses the outlet and the hose behind it. Some coaches have one valve that releases both at once.

Should I leave the black tank valve open at a full hookup?

No. Left open, the liquid drains away as you use the toilet and the solids stay behind with nothing to carry them out, which is what builds the mound that blocks the tank and coats the sensors. Keep the valve closed and let the tank fill before dumping.

Enzyme treatment or chemical treatment?

They do different jobs and they interfere with each other. Enzyme and bacteria products break waste and paper down and need a clean tank to work, because residue from a chemical treatment kills them. A tank that has run on chemicals wants cleaning before an enzyme product will do much.

What must never go into the black tank?

Anything that does not dissolve. That means feminine hygiene products, paper towels and wipes, and household toilet paper that resists breaking down. Undiluted bleach, caustic drain openers and automotive antifreeze also damage the seals and the tank, and a formaldehyde-based product is best left out.
