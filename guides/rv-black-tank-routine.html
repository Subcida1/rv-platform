RV black tank routine: dump, treat, store, and diagnose

Four steps and one habit cover almost everything a black tank asks of you. Dump with the valve closed and the tank mostly full. Treat after every dump. Store it drained and protected, with antifreeze in the tank and the bowl flushed dry. And when something goes wrong, check the gauge before buying anything, because a tank that reads full when it is empty is usually reporting residue rather than a failed sensor.

By OriginRV. Last updated on 2026-10-10.

The short version: four steps, in this order. Dump with the black valve closed until the tank is mostly full, then black first and gray second. Treat after every dump, and give the treatment water to work in. Store it drained and protected, with antifreeze in the tank and the bowl flushed dry. And when the panel says full on a tank you emptied yesterday, diagnose before you buy anything, because that is usually residue rather than a failed sensor.

Start here: the gauge that reads full on an empty tank

This is the complaint that sends most people looking for parts, and it usually is not a broken part. Factory tank sensors are conductivity probes mounted through the tank wall. When liquid touches a probe it completes a circuit and the panel lights that level. Waste, toilet paper fibre or soap film left on a probe completes the same circuit with the tank nearly empty, which is why a panel that reads a third or two thirds on a freshly dumped tank is almost always reporting residue rather than depth.

So the first move is a clean, not a purchase: fill the tank partway with water, add a treatment that targets paper, let it sit for the time the label gives, drive if you can so the motion works the residue loose, and dump when the tank is mostly full. The black tank flush helps as well. If the reading survives that, the tank sensor guide separates a fouled sensor from a wiring fault with two tests, and covers what cleaning cannot reach.

Dump

Dump with the black tank valve closed and the tank allowed to fill, because the volume of liquid behind the waste is what carries it out. Emptying a part-full tank moves the liquid and leaves the solids. When you do dump, it is black first and gray second, so the gray water rinses the outlet and the hose behind the black water.

The valve stays closed at a full hookup. Left open, the liquid drains away as you use the toilet and the solids stay behind with nothing to carry them, which is how a tank builds the mound that later blocks it and coats the sensors. The black tank guide has the seven-step dump procedure, the water volumes Jayco's manual gives, the tank flush, and the tank heaters.

Treat

Dose the tank after every dump, and treat the two families of product as different tools. Enzyme and bacteria treatments break waste and paper into smaller particles, and they need a clean tank to work: residue from a chemical treatment kills the enzymes, so a tank that has had chemicals in it wants cleaning before an enzyme product will do its job. Chemical treatments do their own work by a different route.

What never goes in is anything that does not dissolve: feminine hygiene products, paper towels and wipes, and household toilet paper that resists breaking down. Undiluted bleach, caustic drain openers and automotive antifreeze damage the seals and the tank, and a formaldehyde-based product is best left out of one. The black tank guide carries the wording from Jayco's manual and from Thetford on each of those, and the toilet guide covers what a bowl that will not hold water means for the seal.

Store

Before the first freeze, empty both waste tanks and put RV antifreeze down the toilet into the black tank. Only RV antifreeze goes in: automotive antifreeze is toxic, and antifreeze must never reach the fresh water system, the water heater, the water filter or the refrigerator. A coach with a macerator gets antifreeze run through the pump until it discharges at the nozzle.

While the coach is in use, keep water in the toilet bowl: that bowl is the vapor barrier between the tank and the room, and a dry bowl is a straight path for tank air. Once it is stored for winter the bowl is flushed dry and gets antifreeze like the rest of the system. The winterizing guide covers the plumbing side, and the black tank guide has the tank-specific steps, including the tank heaters and blowing out a black tank flush line.

Why does my black tank read full when it is empty?

Because factory sensors measure conductivity, not depth. Residue left on a probe completes the circuit with the tank nearly empty. Clean the tank and the probes first: a part-full fill of water with a paper-targeting treatment, time, motion if you can drive, and a dump at a mostly full tank. If the reading survives that, the sensor guide's two tests separate a fouled sensor from a wiring fault.

In what order do I dump the tanks?

Black first, then gray. The gray water follows the black through the same hose and rinses the outlet and the hose behind it. Some coaches have one valve that releases both at once.

Should I leave the black tank valve open at a full hookup?

No. Left open, the liquid drains away as you use the toilet and the solids stay behind with nothing to carry them out, which is what builds the mound that blocks the tank and coats the sensors. Keep the valve closed and let the tank fill before dumping.

Enzyme treatment or chemical treatment?

They do different jobs and they interfere with each other. Enzyme and bacteria products break waste and paper down and need a clean tank to work, because residue from a chemical treatment kills them. A tank that has run on chemicals wants cleaning before an enzyme product will do much.

What must never go into the black tank?

Anything that does not dissolve. That means feminine hygiene products, paper towels and wipes, and household toilet paper that resists breaking down. Undiluted bleach, caustic drain openers and automotive antifreeze also damage the seals and the tank, and a formaldehyde-based product is best left out.
