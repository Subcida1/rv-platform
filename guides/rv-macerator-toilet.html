RV macerator toilet not flushing: the hum, the clog, and the power

A macerating toilet is not a gravity toilet with a tank behind it. It has a pump that grinds the waste and pushes it somewhere else, which is why it can sit anywhere in the coach, and it is why the failure sounds and behaves nothing like the toilet on the next page.

By OriginRV. Last updated on Oct 2, 2026.

The short version: a macerating toilet has a pump, and the pump is what the fault sounds like. If it hums and the bowl will not empty, the maker's table starts with a full waste tank and then a clog at the pump inlet, and both come before you suspect anything electrical. If water arrives but the pump does not run, Thetford names exactly two causes: the motor or the control panel. If it flushes and then the bowl drains dry, nothing is broken. The discharge hose has been pulled down and is siphoning, and straightening it fixes it.

Start here: does it hum, and does the bowl empty

Owners describe a macerator fault by its sound rather than by its mechanism. "It just hums" is the most common way this gets written down, and it is a useful description, because a pump that is running and moving nothing is a different problem from a pump that is silent. Decide which of these three you have before touching a part, because they have three different answers and only one of them is electrical.

Worth knowing while you decide whether this page is even yours: Thetford draws the line itself. "A permanent toilet uses gravity to evacuate waste into the holding tanks while a macerating toilet generally have a pump with a macerator that breaks down and pushes waste into the holding tanks." If your toilet has a pedal and no pump noise at all, the gravity toilet page is the right one.

The three faults, and which one you have

It hums and the bowl will not empty. The pump runs, the waste does not move. That is a blockage, and the maker's troubleshooting table starts at the pump inlet.

Water arrives and the pump does not run. The bowl fills and nothing else happens. Thetford's table gives this combination two causes: the motor, or the control panel. That is the table's entry for one symptom rather than a complete diagnosis, which is why the voltage check below comes first.

It flushes, and then the bowl drains dry. That is not a fault. It is siphonage, and it is the one item here that costs nothing.

It hums and the bowl will not empty

The free checks come first

The maker's table has two entries before it reaches anything electrical, and both are free. The first is "waste tank is full", with the instruction to empty it before using the toilet again, so check the tank and its gauge before you touch the toilet at all. The second is "clog at pump inlet", and the instruction is to clear the clog. That ordering is the point: the tank check and the inlet both come ahead of the motor, ahead of the panel and ahead of the wiring, which is the opposite of how most people diagnose a pump that is making noise.

Before you open anything, two warnings apply. The one people do not expect is biological: Thetford's own words are "human fecal wastewater contains substances that can be hazardous to your health, such as bacteria, viruses and fungi," and its instruction is to "always treat fecal wastewater from human activities as hazardous liquid, and use appropriate personal protection equipment when performing work in areas contaminated by wastewater." Gloves, eye protection, and somewhere to wash afterwards. Thetford's words for the parts below the bowl valve are "there is a serious injury risk when touching the sharp macerator knives located below the toilet bowl valve", followed by "use extreme caution when removing obstructions out of the macerator house". The macerator is disabled while the bowl valve is open, which is the maker's stated protection, and the manual's order for an obstruction is to open the bowl valve, switch off the power at the control board, and then remove the blockage. What this page tells you is the opposite, and it has no exceptions: do not open the bowl valve and do not put a hand anywhere near the macerator until the power is isolated at the control board. The maker’s servicing rule puts obstruction removal with an approved competent person, and a blockage is where that rule earns its keep, because the blades below the valve are the reason the warning exists. The manual's order assumes an interlock that is working on a toilet whose fault you have not identified yet, and the same servicing rule puts this work with an approved competent person. A macerator you have not isolated is where this page stops.

A solid object in the macerator

If the inlet is clear and the pump still will not move waste, the next entry is "solid object in macerator", and the remedy is in three parts: disable the power, attempt to remove the object, and if that is unsuccessful, contact Thetford. That third part is not a formality. It is the manual telling you where the owner's work stops.

There is a second symptom worth knowing because it points at the same cause: if the toilet has become noisy, the 2015 Tecma manual says the pump may be partially blocked if a solid object other than toilet paper has been flushed. A partially blocked pump is one explanation the manual gives for unusual noise, and it is a separate entry from the pumping check above.

Where this stops being owner work

Thetford recommends that plumbing and electrical work be performed by a licensed tradesperson, and its own troubleshooting guide describes itself as a basic service aid rather than a repair procedure, adding that "if the suggested actions above do not resolve the issue, it may be necessary to bring unit in for professional service". Past clearing a blockage with the power off, the honest answer is a technician. If you would rather hand it over, the directory lists mobile technicians and repair centres by state, and many of them work on macerating toilets at your site.

Water arrives but the pump does not run

The maker names two causes: the motor or the control panel

The troubleshooting table entry reads "water added, but pump fails to flush", and the cause it gives is short: a potential problem with the motor or the control panel. Two candidates, and the manual does not pick between them for you, which is the point at which diagnosis needs a meter and a wiring diagram rather than a description from a page.

Before either: voltage, and the 10 percent drop

There is one measurement worth taking first, because it is free and it is in the same manual. The supply should be 12 volts plus or minus 2, and there should be no more than a 10 percent decrease in voltage while the macerator is running. If it drops further than that, the manual's conclusion is a wiring problem in the RV rather than a fault in the toilet, so the fault you are chasing is in the coach rather than in the toilet.

And one thing the same manual does not settle. Thetford's two documents for this product family disagree about the circuit, and neither this page nor the manual resolves the difference:

  • The 2015 Tecma manual states that "every toilet requires a 12-VDC/40-AMP dedicated circuit with 8-gauge wire and 40-AMP breaker or fuse between the main bus or battery and a terminal located near the toilet".
  • The later Silence Plus 2G manual carries a wiring chart headed "12 VOLTS - Install", giving 10, 8 or 6 gauge wire by run length, against a 30 amp fuse where the 2015 manual specifies 40.

Both are Thetford documents, eight years apart, and a 40 amp circuit and a 30 amp one are not the same claim. If you still have the manual that came with the toilet, that is the one to wire against. If you do not, the maker publishes its manuals by model, so establishing which model you have is the step that decides this rather than picking whichever of the two figures above sounds safer, and the later document carries a fire warning for exactly that mistake. Both documents do treat the toilet as its own circuit, and the later one spells it out: "every toilet must have its own circuit breaker or fuse." It attaches a fire hazard warning to using the recommended rating rather than something else: "always use recommended circuit breakers or fuses. Failure to follow the recommendations can result in fire." A macerating toilet is not a load to share a circuit with something else.

It flushes, then the bowl drains dry

The discharge hose has been pulled down

This one reads like a fault and is not. The manual's entry is "bowl drains dry after flush", and its cause is "water is siphoning out of bowl due to discharge hose pulled down". The remedy is to straighten the hose so that the top of the discharge line is in line with the toilet nozzle.

Why it matters that this is here. A bowl that will not hold water is the classic symptom of a failed seal on a gravity toilet, and it is what sends people looking for a part. On a macerating toilet the same symptom can be a hose that has settled, been pushed down behind the toilet, or been re-routed during something else. That is a five minute fix, and it is the reason this check sits above anything involving a part.

Two figures from the installation instructions, for anyone re-routing that hose. The Silence Plus 2G manual specifies a 1.5 inch internal diameter sanitation hose or pipe, allows rigid PVC, and asks that flexible hose avoid sharp bends that could kink it, and the same manual says the toilet should ideally sit higher than the holding tank with a 6 foot rise the maximum that will not affect performance. Thetford's support page for that toilet gives a 12 volt macerator pump a total pumping distance of up to 120 feet, which is the whole run rather than the rise, so a long horizontal run eats into it once the 6 foot rise is counted. Those are the maker's figures for those models rather than for the whole range, so hose diameter, rise and run are worth checking against the manual for the toilet actually installed.

What must never go in

The maker's own list is specific, and it is worth reading once because it is the cause of the fault most likely to happen again: "only human waste and toilet paper should be put into the toilet. Never flush foreign materials (e.g., paper towels, pre-moistened wipes, condoms, feminine hygiene products, dental floss, household garbage, etc.)."

The wipe sentence is the one people get wrong, and the iNDUS manual is blunter about it than the others: "never use wet wipes, moist toilet tissues or anything similar, even if it says it is safe to flush. Any wet wipe will block the product." For the iNDUS the rule leaves no room at all: any wet wipe will block the product. The same manual adds tampons, hair and cotton swabs to the list.

Cleaning matters for the same reason, and the 2G manual is direct about it: "bleach, petroleum-based products, strong acids and abrasives can cause irreversible damage to the toilet system and components." Its instruction is to "use only non-abrasive, non-petroleum products to clean the toilet." The same manual names household cleaners specifically as a source of both.

Last, one behaviour that reads as a fault and is a design decision. When the tank sensor reads full, the toilet disables the water inlet so the bowl will not fill, and the maker's note is that the macerator pump remains active during that lockout. A toilet that adds no water while the tank gauge is red is working as intended.

What it costs, and what a replacement involves

Nothing on this page has a price attached to it. A figure for a part or a service call that no maker document supports would be a number somebody made up, so the shape of the cost is what this section gives.

At the bottom is the work that costs nothing but a pair of gloves and an hour: clearing a clog at the pump inlet with the power off, straightening a discharge hose that has been pulled down, and reading the voltage while the pump runs. The first two of those are symptom checks rather than faults, and they are here first because they cost nothing. The voltage reading is a measurement to take before the meter work, not a fault in its own right.

In the middle is anything that needs a meter and a decision: a wiring problem, a control panel, or the pump itself. That is work for a technician, and what it costs depends on the fault and the diagnostic time rather than on the price of a part.

At the top is a replacement, and the reason it is at the top is not the price of the toilet. A macerating toilet sits on a discharge line and a circuit, so a replacement can involve either or both, and the maker's servicing rule, that all servicing must be carried out by an approved competent person, is a fair description of why this is not a part you swap yourself. For reference, the manual gives 0.1 to 0.7 gallons per flush, depending on the setting.

Why does my RV macerator toilet hum but not flush?

The pump is running and the waste is not moving, which Thetford's troubleshooting table treats as a blockage first. Its table starts with a full waste tank and then a clog at the pump inlet, both ahead of anything electrical. The manual's own order for an obstruction is to open the bowl valve, switch off the power at the control board, and then remove the blockage, and it carries a serious injury warning about the sharp macerator knives below the bowl valve. Isolate the power at the control board before you open that valve or reach below it. That is the instruction whatever order the manual prints.

My RV toilet fills with water but will not flush. What is wrong?

Thetford gives that combination exactly two causes: "water added, but pump fails to flush" means a potential problem with the motor or the control panel. Before either, check the supply: it should be 12 volts plus or minus 2, with no more than a 10 percent drop while the macerator runs. A bigger drop than that points at the wiring rather than the toilet.

Why does my RV toilet bowl drain after flushing?

On a macerating toilet that is usually siphonage rather than a failed seal. The manual's cause is a discharge hose pulled down, drawing water out of the bowl, and the fix is to straighten the hose so the top of the discharge line is level with the toilet nozzle. It is the cheapest correct repair on this page.

What should never go into an RV macerating toilet?

Only human waste and toilet paper. Thetford lists paper towels, pre-moistened wipes, condoms, feminine hygiene products, dental floss and household garbage as prohibited, and its iNDUS manual is explicit that wet wipes block the product even when they are labelled flushable. Anything that passes through the bowl intact reaches the pump, and that is the fault this page exists to explain.
