Your RV roof can hold snow, it's the ice that wins

Most RV roofs are engineered for a surprising snow load. The failures come from the edges: ice dams working into lap seals, skate-sharp icicles at the seam, and the membrane punctured by a well-meaning shovel. Here's the sane winter approach.

Last reviewed: Sep 16, 2026, for the 2026-27 winter. We update these guides when real-world data changes.

The short version: do the roof walk and sealant pass in October, keep vents and gutters clear, don't climb up onto ice, and never scrape the membrane with metal. If it's a foot or two of fresh snow, your roof is usually fine, it's the repeated freeze-thaw at the edges that kills seals.

The roof pass (do it before it snows)

1. Walk the roof while it's dry

October walk beats February find. TPO and EPDM roofs take light walking fine (no shoes with metal hooks). Look for:

  • Cracks, fish mouths, or lifting in the membrane seams.
  • Lap sealant (the goo at every fixture) that's cracked, peeled, or gone chalky, this is what actually seals your roof penetrations.
  • Screws backing out around vent boots and AC gaskets.
  • Bubbles or delamination on a covered-seam repair, winter will pry these open.

2. Re-seal the weak points now

Two products, two jobs: lap sealant (Dicor-style self-leveling) over the fasteners and fixture flanges, and self-leveling sealant for seams. Clean the area with the roof-safe cleaner first, let it fully dry, then run a bead. One tube is cheap; the leak it prevents is not. Mark a calendar note: sealant life is ~2 seasons regardless of what the tube says.

3. Clear what snow will collect on

Leaves and debris hold water against the membrane all winter, a single season of that petri dish ages the roof fast. Sweep the roof, unblock gutters and downspouts, and make sure the fridge vent and AC returns are clear so you don't trap moisture inside the bays.

When the snow comes

How much is too much?

Fresh snow is light (~5 lb per cubic foot); wet spring snow is 4-5× heavier. Most RV roofs handle 1-2 feet of fresh snow fine. The danger zone is ponding, if heavy snow is turning to water and pooling against the membrane or on a cover, remove the load. A sagging, drum-tight cover is the sign to act. If you ever have to move the rig in snow country, first confirm the loaded weight is inside the 80% rule, snow adds tons faster than it looks.

Ice dams are the real enemy, at the edges

Slush melts down the roof, hits the cold eaves, refreezes into a dam, and the meltwater backs up behind it, under the lap sealant and into the corner framing. What to do:

  • Don't chop at the dam with anything metal.
  • If you must act, use a plastic roof rake at the eaves, pulling snow away from the gutter line so melt has a path off.
  • On asphalt/TPO roofs, warm water (not hot) along the eave works when it's near freezing.
  • Whoever parked the rig: the high-slope side sheds, the low-slope side is where the dam forms. Level the rig so the roof actually drains.

The one thing to never do

Walk up on ice with any tool in hand. Slipping on a frozen membrane punctures it where you land, and you can't see the wound until spring water finds the inside wall. If it's icy, leave it; let the thaw handle it. Your roof's real job is surviving the season, not looking clear in February.

Do I need a snow rake for my RV roof?

Only if you get persistent heavy snow AND have a low-slope roof. A soft-edge plastic roof rake used from a ladder, at the eaves, pulling away from the gutter, never scraping the membrane, is the safe tool. Most owners never need one.

What about a cover with snow on top?

Snow on a cover is fine; ice on a cover is a problem, it abrades and can tear through in wind. Check that the cover can't sag onto the membrane under load, and unbuckle straps before deep snow collects.

Will a heater under the rig melt a snow dam?

Not reliably, and running one unattended for that purpose is the wrong tool. Draining properly and letting the roof shed is the answer; a dam that persists all season still drains at the ends, and your October sealant pass is what actually protects the corners.
