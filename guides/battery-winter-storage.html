RV battery care in winter storage

A dead battery bank in spring is almost never "old age" — it's storage errors from four months earlier. The fix is chemistry-specific and takes twenty minutes.

Last reviewed: Sep 16, 2026 — for the 2026–27 winter. We update these guides when real-world data changes.

The short version: charge to the right level for your chemistry, disconnect everything, and check voltage every few weeks. A discharged lead-acid battery can freeze at around 20°F; a fully charged one is safe to around -60°F. Storage temperature starts with your state of charge.

Chemistry cheat sheet

Flooded lead-acid — charge to 100%, then disconnect

A full charge is your freeze protection. Top up electrolyte with distilled water first (after charging, not before — acid expands), then:

  • Charge to 100%.
  • Disconnect the negative terminal — the whole reason batteries die over winter is parasitic drain from the controller, clock modules, and anything left on.
  • Clean the terminals and coat lightly with dielectric grease or petroleum jelly.
  • Re-check voltage every 4–6 weeks; if it reads under 12.4V, charge it again. (While the battery is out, it's the perfect moment to verify your RV weight limits against what you actually carry.)

LiFePO4 (lithium) — store at 50–80%, never charge below freezing

Lithium's rules are inverted. Charging below ~32°F causes lithium plating — permanent, cumulative capacity loss — so if your rig sits where it can freeze, don't leave the charger or solar connected with the battery below freezing.

  • Target 50–80% state of charge for storage (per your BMS manual — most lithium vendors publish one).
  • Disconnect to kill parasitic draw — lithium holds charge for months, but a controller still drains it.
  • Never charge while the cell temperature is below freezing. If your BMS has low-temp cutoff, confirm it's enabled.
  • A full charge is fine if the bank stays above freezing and disconnected — but mid-range is kinder to the cells for long sits.

AGM / gel (sealed lead-acid) — treat like flooded, minus the water

Full charge, disconnect, check every 4–6 weeks. No watering, same parasitic-drain rules, same 80% rule on the deep-discharge side in spring.

The storage checklist

  □ Charge to the target for your chemistry (lead: 100%; lithium: 50–80%).
  □ Top-off / maintain flooded lead-acid before the final charge.
  □ Disconnect the negative cable (note: label which cable is which before you pull it).
  □ Turn off or disconnect the solar controller and inverter.
  □ Clean terminals; protect with grease.
  □ Record the date. Re-check voltage every 4–6 weeks.
  □ If you use a maintainer, use a smart one — never a dumb charger left on a lithium pack.

The rule that saves most people

It is almost never the cold that kills the battery. It's the parasitic drain leaving it half-full in January. Disconnect is the whole game.

Can I leave the batteries in the rig all winter?

Yes — in the rig is fine, as long as they're charged/disconnected per chemistry above and the compartment doesn't fill with snowmelt. Disconnected is the requirement; removal is optional.

What voltage means "still fine"?

Lead-acid: above 12.4V resting. Below 12.2V, top it up. Lithium: check per BMS app or manual — resting voltage alone misleads on LiFePO4.

Why does my solar controller drain the battery if the panels are covered?

Controllers draw a little just being on, and covered panels may not cover the draw in cloudy weeks. Combine: disconnect the battery, or set the controller to storage mode if it has one.
