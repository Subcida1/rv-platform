# Competitive landscape — OriginRV (research session, 2026-10-15)

Digest of the three reports Neo sent (monetization strategies, authority case studies, campground-finder analysis) plus an independent verification and extension pass. The three reports concluded "no central RV repair authority exists" and treated the space as vacant. **It is not vacant.** This is what the search actually returns.

## 1. Verification of the load-bearing claims

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Mediavine Journey = 1,000 sessions | **VERIFIED** — Mediavine's own help page and Journey's Zendesk: "At least 1,000 premium sessions over a 30-day period". |
| Mediavine Official = $5,000/yr ad revenue | **VERIFIED** — same help page. |
| Raptive = ~100,000 pageviews | **STALE** — Raptive cut it to **25,000 monthly pageviews** (announced 2025-10-20; Search Engine Journal + Raptive's own eligibility page). Report 1 quotes the pre-2025 rule. |
| Journey accepts AI-assisted sites | **CAUTION** — Journey's own disqualifier list includes "extreme brand safety concerns or **excessive use of AI**". AI-assisted is fine; AI-reading is not. |
| RVIA 2026 forecast ~349,300 units | not re-verified this session (report cites RVIA RoadSigns; treat as directional). |

## 2. The direct competitors the three reports missed

A search for OriginRV's exact positioning — symptom-first repair guides plus a repair directory — returns a crowded field.

**Repair-content competitors (symptom-first guides):**

- **CampWrench** (campwrench.com/fixes) — the closest structural match: "Pick the symptom that matches what your rig is doing. Each guide starts with the answer, then walks the diagnostic order a technician would use, cheapest and safest test first." Organized by system, with per-guide difficulty / time / "N likely causes". **Thin: ~13 guides** against OriginRV's 58; no visible sources; templated cause counts.
- **RV Solver** (rvsolver.com/guides) — "RV repair guides organized by system... understand how RV systems work before replacing parts." Same shape.
- **Campwright** (rv.tudiencau.com) — "Fix it at the campsite... the checks you can do before you call a mobile tech." Note the domain: a subdirectory of an unrelated domain, a content-farm signature.
- **SMART-RV** (smart-rv.com) — the most ambitious: "The Largest RV Repair Database & Verified Tech Directory" — peer-reviewed field cases logged by RVTI/NRVTA-certified techs (two-tech review before publishing), plus a credential-verified tech directory. **This is OriginRV's stated positioning, executed from the technician side.**
- **TheRVgeeks** (thervgeeks.com) — 800+ articles, 300+ videos, "no AI!", 20 years full-time. The incumbent authority.
- **The RV Repair Manual** (rvrepairmanual.com), **RVers Corner** (rverscorner.com) — older-school, retired/working techs.
- **iFixit's RV section** — exists but is thin (5 categories, one author). The iFixit playbook is the model; iFixit itself has not executed it for RVs.

**Directory competitors (the bigger surprise):**

- **RV Service List** (rvservicelist.com) — **4,557 techs/shops across 2,199 towns in 49 states** against OriginRV's 2,027, with **555 published rate cards** ("copied from the tech's own page with the date it was read"), town-level pages A–Z, a cost guide, and the same "no booking fee, no platform taking a cut" pitch. **This is OriginRV's directory, larger, with a differentiator OriginRV does not have: published rates.**
- **RV Help** (rvhelp.com) — "The Trusted Directory for Mobile RV Repair & RV Inspection", 2,500+ credential-vetted providers across 48 states, partnered with NRVIA and RVTAA, requiring NRVTA or RVTI Level 2. Credential-vetting is its moat.
- **RV Repair Near Me** (rvrepairnearme.net) — claims 10,000+ verified shops and runs "SEO Boost partners — signed-up shops with dedicated landing pages": **it already sells the paid-placement model OriginRV plans.**
- **RVTI's own "Find a Certified Technician"** map — the industry body's directory.
- **FindAnRVTech.com** (RVTI-backed), **Yellow Pages RV** (US + Canada map), **Rigistry** (1,500+), **RVFix** (2,500+ mobile techs, dispatch plus video diagnosis).

## 3. What this changes

1. **"Vacant throne" is wrong; "fragmented and shallow" is right.** The repair-content competitors are thin (13–50 guides, no sourcing discipline). The directory competitors are broad but undifferentiated. OriginRV's edge is **depth plus sourcing discipline**, not first-mover advantage.
2. **The directory is not a moat by itself.** 4,557 listings beat 2,027 today. What OriginRV has that they do not: 50 states (RV Service List has 49), per-listing quotes and verification, and a content engine feeding it.
3. **Rate transparency is the live differentiator in the directory space** (RV Service List's 555 rate cards). OriginRV has none.
4. **Credential-vetting is the other** (RV Help, SMART-RV). OriginRV has none.
5. **SMART-RV is the strategic threat to watch** — it attempts exactly OriginRV's positioning from the credential side, which is the harder side to copy.

## 4. Threats

- **AI Overviews on informational queries** — the guides are informational; tools and the directory are the resilient surfaces.
- **Journey's "excessive use of AI" disqualifier** — the ad path depends on the site not reading as AI-generated.
- **RV Life's rollup** (iRV2, brand forums, CampgroundReviews, Trip Wizard, DoItYourselfRV, MaintainMyRV) — it owns the audience and could add a repair directory.
- **Camping World × RepairPal** — a national repair network with standardized pricing; if it opens consumer-facing lead gen it competes for the same shop relationships.
- **The thin competitors are AI-fast** — CampWrench and its peers can add fifty guides in a week. OriginRV's defense is sourcing and review, which they are not doing.

## 5. Prioritized hit list

1. **Grow the directory and add rate transparency.** The competitive floor is 4,557 listings, and "published rate plus date read" is a differentiator a competitor already claims. Add a rate field to the listing record.
2. **Add a credential/verification field** (RVTI, NRVTA, RVIA). It is the other live differentiator, and it is what SMART-RV and RV Help use to claim trust.
3. **Apply to Mediavine Journey** at 1,000 sessions — while keeping the content reading as human-sourced (the AI disqualifier).
4. **Ship the embeddable tools.** The reports' number one, and the one channel no competitor in this field is using.
5. **Publish the annual "State of RV Repair" report.** Nobody here publishes one; the directory plus rate data is the seed.
6. **Watch SMART-RV.** If it gains traction, the "verified tech" positioning is taken.
