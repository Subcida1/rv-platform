# One search box that understands: site search, places, and the opening element

Settled with T on 2026-10-30. This is the destination doc for the search rework and the
homepage opening element. It supersedes the "Known search limitation" note in
`~/.hermes/skills/originrv-site-ops` section 10 and the two-door layout described in the
`index.html` comments.

Nothing here is built yet. T says "go" before any of it lands.

## Why this exists

T typed `los angeles` into the homepage "Where are you" field and the site could not find it.
The investigation found the cause is not a typo, a wiring bug, or a broken script. The field
was never a search box.

Measured on 2026-10-30:

- `assets/js/locate.js` resolves a typed town by scanning the `loc` string of the 2,022
  business records in `assets/js/search-index.js`. There is no place table behind it.
- Only 4 of 2,022 businesses name Los Angeles anywhere. Chicago: **zero**. Miami: one.
- A typed town becomes a substring filter on the state page (`?loc=`), not a location, so
  the reader lands on a text-filtered list of four records instead of shops sorted by
  distance.
- On zero or one match the field navigates or bounces to `/directory/` with no explanation.

The Census place file (`_data/source/2024_Gaz_place_national.txt`, 32,333 places) and the
ZCTA centroid file (`_data/source/2024_Gaz_zcta_national.txt`, 33,791 ZIP areas with
lat/lon) are already in the repo and are used only offline to generate per-state coordinate
tables. They were never consulted for a typed location.

## The decisions

### D1. One search box on the page. The tech field comes out.
The homepage carries three things shaped like a search. The nav field and the hero field are
the same search and stay. The "I need a tech" input is **removed**; that element becomes a
CTA that links to the directory.

One box, two lookups underneath. One lookup was rejected: it would mean ranking pages and
places in the same pass and would be slower and worse at both.

### D2. The one box understands content and places.
Typing a symptom, a part, a maker, or a model searches page content. Typing a town, a
neighbourhood, a ZIP, or "city, state" resolves a place and offers the directory. The reader
never chooses a mode; the box decides from what was typed.

### D3. The opening element is rebuilt, not reshuffled.
Order on screen, top to bottom:

1. **Search box**, first. A person with a problem searches first.
2. **One full-width primary door:** "Something is broken" leading to the 60 troubleshoot
   guides, with the promise in plain words (what to test, in what order).
3. **One compact secondary strip**, not an equal card: "Need a tech instead? Mobile techs and
   shops in all 50 states," linking to the directory.

Why: two equal-weight door cards is the pattern the design doctrine rejects. The primary job
of the top of the page is "something is broken, help me fix it," because that is what the
site's strongest content serves. The directory is the second decision people make, after
they decide they cannot fix it themselves.

The three-number strip stays where it is unless the rebuild pushes it below the fold, in
which case it moves inside the primary door's section rather than being cut.

### D4. Places are towns, neighbourhoods, and ZIPs. Never street addresses.
Accepted input:
- **Towns and cities**, including "city, state" and "city ST".
- **Any 5-digit ZIP**, resolved by nearest ZIP-area centroid, so a ZIP with no boundary or a
  spot between towns still resolves.
- **Neighbourhoods** that are not Census places resolve through their ZIP area, which is a
  few miles off at worst.
- **Address-shaped input** has the town and ZIP extracted from it and resolves on those. The
  suggestion says what it used ("Using Boise, ID"), so nothing is silently discarded.

Street addresses do not resolve from house numbers. That is a promise problem, not a data
problem: a house number cannot tell us which side of a town line or a mountain the reader is
on, so a resolution would be a guess presented as an answer.

The field's helper line becomes **"Town, neighbourhood, or ZIP"**. The old "ZIP or town"
is what narrowed the reader's expectation to two things in the first place.

### D5. Misspellings and abbreviations resolve.
- A misspelling of a place we already know about ("los angelas") resolves, with "Showing
  results for Los Angeles" above the suggestions.
- Common abbreviations for large places resolve ("LA", "Ft Worth", "St Lou").
- Anything vaguer gets a "did you mean" rather than a silent wrong guess.

### D6. No dead ends, and no capability we do not have.
- **Place with listings:** directory results, distance-sorted, with towns the business
  covers included.
- **Place without listings:** the nearest state page, plus the honest sentence that no shops
  are listed in that town yet. Never a silent bounce to a generic hub.
- **Nothing matches anywhere:** the dropdown says what was searched and offers two real next
  clicks, the guides list and the repair directory. A DIY route first, because a reader who
  typed a symptom usually wants a fix.
- **Thin areas are tracked, not advertised.** A monthly report lists towns that were searched
  and returned few results, so research hours go where the demand is. Nothing on a public
  page says the site is thin, because that contradicts the authority claim.

### D7. One matching engine underneath, each surface chooses its corpus.
The nav and hero dropdown, the manuals hub, the directory finder, and the parts filter all
use the shared matcher for typos and word matching. Each surface still decides what it
searches. The parts filter (`assets/js/parts.js`) currently does a raw substring filter with
no typo tolerance and moves onto the shared rule.

### D8. Acceptance is 100 real terms, run in a browser, kept in the build gate.
The gate is permanent and runs in `ci.sh`, not a one-time QA pass, so this cannot rot.

| Group | Count | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Symptom and part queries in customer words | 40 | Each reaches a guide or tool |
| Maker and model terms | 15 | Each reaches a manual or guide |
| Places with listings | 20 | Directory results, distance-sorted |
| Places with no listings | 10 | Honest thin-area answer, nearest state page |
| Misspellings and abbreviations | 10 | Resolve, with the correction stated |
| Address-shaped inputs | 5 | Town extracted and named |
| Empty, one character, junk, emoji, 40-word paragraph | 5 | No throw, no dead end |

Every term carries its expected outcome beside it. The run happens against the served pages
in a real browser, not just the JS functions.

A second check asserts that **every town named by a listing resolves through the resolver**.
That is the check that would have caught this week's defect, because it derives its inputs
from the shipped data rather than a hand-kept list.

## What was built on 2026-10-30, and what changed from this plan

Built and verified on branch `preview/search`, not yet merged:

- `scripts/build-places.py` and `assets/js/places.js`: 24,747 place keys, 33,791 real ZIP areas
  (as a 100,000 character bitstring), 172 KB gzipped against a 180 KB budget. `--selftest` pins
  Indianapolis (`F`), Baton Rouge (`B`), all of Hawaii (`S`), Boise (`Boise City city`), the
  Michigan charter townships, and Los Angeles and Chicago.
- `assets/js/locate.js` rewritten as `RV_LOCATE`. `finder.js` and `search.js` both call it.
- `scripts/check-search.js`: 110 real queries in seven groups plus every listing base town,
  wired into `ci.sh` with its self-test first. It runs in about a second.
- The zero-result panel now carries two real next clicks (guides, directory), and thin towns
  get the honest "no shop listed here yet" line.

**Three things changed from the plan above, each for a measured reason:**

1. **The ZIP centroid table is not shipped.** Measured: 146 of 400 random mainland points are
   more than 30 miles from their nearest ZIP centroid, worst case 659 miles, because a point in
   water has no nearby ZIP. A typed ZIP resolves by its state instead (exact and free), and the
   real ZCTA list ships as a bitstring so a nonexistent ZIP is refused rather than answered by
   its prefix. That is 10 KB instead of 80, and it fixes "99999 = Alaska".
2. **The budget is 180 KB, not 150.** The table carries every incorporated city plus all
   33,791 ZIP areas. The number is enforced, so growth has to be a decision.
3. **"huston" is not a misspelling of Houston.** It is a real Pennsylvania township of 64 square
   miles. Five attempts to correct it to Houston each broke a different real place (Cortez to
   Porter, Candler to Chandler, Boutte to Butte). The rule that survived is the boring one: if
   the table carries the name, answer it.

**One data defect found and left for T:** "Reno-Tahoe Mobile RV Repair" is filed under
California with base "Stateline", and its own description says it is based at Stateline,
Nevada. The search gate reports it as a note rather than a failure, because the record is
wrong and the resolver is right.

**One content gap recorded rather than papered over:** the site has nothing on LiFePO4 or
lithium batteries, so "lifepo4" correctly reaches the zero-result panel. The test that used to
require an answer now uses a query the corpus does answer, and the gap is written in a comment
where a content pass will find it.


Order, because each step depends on the one above it.

1. **`scripts/build-places.py`** emits `assets/js/places.js`: every Census place name mapped
   to the states that carry it (~70 KB gzipped), plus the ZCTA centroid table for nearest-ZIP
   resolution (~165 KB gzipped). Generated, `--check` mode, wired into `check-generated.sh`
   and `ci.sh` the same way `build-coords.py` is.
2. **`assets/js/locate.js` becomes `RV_LOCATE`**, the single resolver: ZIP, exact town, town
   plus state suffix, prefix and fuzzy fallback, address extraction, and a typed result
   (`place`, `thin`, `none`) instead of a bare URL. The state-suffix stripping and name
   canonicalising already in `assets/js/finder.js` move here so both surfaces share them.
3. **`assets/js/search.js`** asks `RV_LOCATE` for a place answer and ranks it against page
   content. The place result goes first when the input really is a place.
4. **`assets/js/site.js`** injects the place table lazily on first focus, the pattern already
   used for the ZIP table. The 165 KB never loads for a reader who does not type a location.
5. **`index.html`** hero rebuilt to D3. The tech form is deleted; the directory becomes a CTA
   link.
6. **`assets/js/parts.js`** moves onto the shared matcher.
7. **`scripts/check-search.js`** holds the 100 terms and the listing-town check. Self-test
   first in `ci.sh`, negative-tested by reintroducing the Los Angeles defect and confirming it
   names the term.
8. **`scripts/thin-areas.py`** reads recorded search terms and writes the monthly report into
   `_reports/`.

## Decided against

- **A third-party search service** (Algolia, Typesense, Meilisearch, Pagefind). They search
  site content; the defect was a place lookup. A vendor would not have fixed Los Angeles. Our
  corpus is 2,166 entries, so the matcher is not the weak link.
- **A separate place box.** One box that decides from the input (D1, D2).
- **Street-address geocoding.** Needs a paid geocoder and still guesses (D4).
- **Ranking pages and places in one pass.** One box, two lookups (D1).
- **Visible "we need providers here" notices.** Undermines the authority claim (D6).
- **A one-time QA run instead of a gate.** The terms live in `ci.sh` (D8).

## Out of scope

- Neighbourhood names that are not a Census place and whose ZIP area you do not know. These
  resolve by ZIP, and a hand-kept alias list extends coverage where it matters, the same way
  `_data/place-aliases.json` already works for listing names.
- Live business data, opening hours, or provider recruitment. The thin-area report tells us
  where to look; finding providers stays a research task.
