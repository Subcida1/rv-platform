New Hampshire's White Mountains are the draw, and the RV season follows the summer and the foliage. There are two kinds of RV repair. The difference is whether you can move your RV. If you cannot, you need a mobile technician who comes to you. If you can drive it, a service center has the bays and parts for bigger jobs. Both are here, along with roadside help from one provider.

What to look for in an RV technician

  • Can it move? That decides everything. If the RV cannot be driven, you need a mobile technician, not a shop. If it can, a center with bays and parts is usually faster for big work.
  • Certification. RVIA and RVDA certifications (Registered Technician through Master Certified), or NRVTA and RVTI training, mean the tech has passed industry exams. Ask what level they hold.
  • Emergency or roadside. For a breakdown on the highway, confirm the business does roadside or mobile dispatch before you count on them.
  • Warranty capability. If your RV is under manufacturer or extended warranty, confirm the shop can do warranty work, or you may pay out of pocket for something covered.
  • LP gas license. Propane system repairs and safety inspections require a licensed LP gas technician in most states.

RV repair directory FAQ

What is the difference between a mobile technician and a service center?

A mobile technician drives to your RV and repairs it where it sits, which is what you need if the RV cannot be moved or you do not want to wait for a shop bay. A service center is a shop you drive to, with the bays, lifts, and parts for bigger jobs like collision repair, roof replacement, and structural work.

What counts as an emergency here?

Those are two different things, not the same call. Roadside means the vehicle is the problem and the RV may not move, so those businesses work on chassis, engine, drivetrain, brakes, or arrange towing. Emergency mobile repair means something inside the coach has failed, like a furnace, water heater, or refrigerator, and a technician comes to your site. The first is for being stranded. The second is for an RV that is parked but unlivable.

Who do I call if my RV will not move?

Choose "my RV will not move" above. Every business there comes to you or dispatches, and works on the vehicle itself rather than only what is inside it. If you need a tow specifically, say so on the call, since not all of them run a tow truck. For a diesel chassis breakdown, a dedicated roadside diesel service is often the fastest option.

How are the results sorted?

By whether they reach your area, then by how close they are to you. A business that works out of your own town, or that names your town or county as somewhere it covers, comes ahead of one that is simply based nearby, because those are the ones that will actually reach you. If a business names no town and no area, it still appears, shown under the region it serves.
