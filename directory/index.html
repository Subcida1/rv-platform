What to look for in an RV technician

  • Can it move? That decides everything. If the rig cannot be driven, you need a mobile technician, not a shop. If it can, a center with bays and parts is usually faster for big work.
  • Certification. RVIA and RVDA certifications (Registered Technician through Master Certified), or NRVTA and RVTI training, mean the tech has passed industry exams. Ask what level they hold.
  • Emergency or roadside. For a breakdown on the highway, confirm the business does roadside or mobile dispatch before you count on them.
  • Warranty capability. If your rig is under manufacturer or extended warranty, confirm the shop can do warranty work, or you may pay out of pocket for something covered.
  • LP gas license. Propane system repairs and safety inspections require a licensed LP gas technician in most states.

RV repair directory FAQ

What is the difference between a mobile technician and a service center?

A mobile technician drives to your rig and repairs it where it sits, which is what you need if the RV cannot be moved or you do not want to wait for a shop bay. A service center is a shop you drive to, with the bays, lifts, and parts for bigger jobs like collision repair, roof replacement, and structural work.

Who do I call if I am broken down on the highway?

Use the Emergency and roadside filter above. Those businesses state that they do roadside, mobile dispatch, or emergency service. For a diesel chassis breakdown, a dedicated roadside diesel service is often the fastest option.
