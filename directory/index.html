How listings get built

Real businesses only, never made up

Every listing in this directory is a real business, added from what the business itself publishes on its own website. We do not invent phone numbers, addresses, or certifications. If a detail is not published by the business, it is not in the listing. A listing marked unclaimed goes to the business the moment they claim it, and they control it from then on.

What to look for in an RV technician

  • Certification. RVIA and RVDA certifications (Registered Technician through Master Certified) mean the tech has passed industry training and exams. Ask what level they hold.
  • Mobile or shop. A mobile technician comes to your rig, which saves towing and lets them see the problem where it lives. A service center has the bays and parts for bigger jobs.
  • Warranty capability. If your rig is under manufacturer or extended warranty, confirm the shop can do warranty work before you book, or you may pay out of pocket for something covered.
  • LP gas license. Propane system repairs and safety inspections require a licensed LP gas technician in most states. If you smell propane, this is the credential that matters.
  • Specialties. Some techs specialize in appliances, solar, generators, or structural work. A good match matters more than a long list.

RV repair directory FAQ

Yes. Listings are free and permanent, and the business controls its own listing after claiming it. There is no commission and no middleman between you and the customer.

A business is listed when it is real and publishes its own contact information. We add nothing we cannot verify from the business itself. Certification badges only appear when the business states the certification.
