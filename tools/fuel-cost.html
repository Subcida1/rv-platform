RV fuel cost calculator

The math is simple: miles divided by your miles per gallon, times the price of fuel. The hard part is knowing the numbers to put in. Every box says where its number comes from. Any vehicle works, RV or car: the RV presets use real-world MPG ranges, the car presets use the average for that kind of vehicle on the road, with the exact figure for any specific model one lookup away.

Fields marked with a star are required. The answer updates as you type.

Your trip

How to get it: put your route into Google Maps and read the total, or add up the legs yourself. If the trip is there and back, enter the round-trip total, since that is the fuel you will buy.
Sets a real-world MPG range for RV types. Car, SUV, pickup, van and motorcycle use the on-road average for that kind of vehicle, since the exact figure for a specific model is published at fueleconomy.gov. Your own measured MPG below always wins over any preset.

Your numbers

How to get it: fill the tank, note the odometer, drive a normal stretch, fill again. Miles driven divided by gallons on the second fill. One tank is a rough answer, three tanks is a good one, and the number moves with wind, weight, and mountains, so measure on roads like the trip you are pricing.
How to get it: the price at your last fill is the best one. Otherwise AAA posts the daily national average for regular and diesel, and GasBuddy shows station prices along your route. Diesel is running well above regular this year, so use the diesel figure if that is what you burn.
Optional, RVs only. Most gasoline motorhome generators burn roughly half a gallon to one gallon per hour under load, so the total is shown as a range. The box only appears for RVs. Leave empty to skip.

Where the MPG figures come from

There are no official fuel economy ratings for RVs. The EPA does not test motorhomes, because their chassis exceed the weight limits of the EPA's fuel economy testing, and towable RVs have no MPG figure at all because the fuel economy belongs to the tow vehicle. So the ranges here come from trade sources that gathered owner reports and dealer figures: Class A gasoline 6 to 8 MPG, Class A diesel 7 to 10, Class C gasoline 8 to 11, Class C diesel 12 to 16, Class B vans 14 to 20, travel trailers 8 to 14, and fifth wheels 8 to 12.

The towable numbers are the tow vehicle's fuel economy while pulling, not a property of the trailer: a half-ton gasoline truck that does 18 MPG empty might do 12 to 14 pulling a travel trailer, and a three-quarter-ton diesel pulling a fifth wheel might do 8 to 12. And the Class B spread is wide because it spans gasoline and diesel vans, where a diesel Sprinter runs near the top of the range and a gasoline ProMaster near the bottom. Your own measured MPG, entered in the box, is better than any of it.

For cars, SUVs, pickups, vans and motorcycles the tool uses the on-road average for that kind of vehicle, from the US Department of Energy's fleet figures: 25.6 MPG for cars, 18.5 for light trucks and vans, and 44 for motorcycles. Those are fleet averages across every age and model, not a promise about yours. The exact figure for any specific car, pickup or SUV is published at fueleconomy.gov, which lists the EPA estimate for every model year back to 1984, so one lookup beats the average every time.

What the answer means

The estimate is the middle of the road, not a promise. Wind, elevation, speed, and how heavy the coach is loaded all move the real number, which is why the result shows what the trip costs at both ends of your MPG range as well as the middle. Camper Smarts, the same method this tool uses, recommends carrying a cushion of 10 to 15 percent over the estimate, and the result applies that for you as the planning figure. Motorhomes and tow vehicles also run most efficiently somewhere in the 55 to 65 MPH band, so a trip planned at 75 burns measurably more than the same trip at 60.

Where to go next

  • RVing Know How, best MPG RVs (updated September 2026): chassis-level MPG figures, which are the reason the Class B range spans gasoline and diesel vans.

  • AAA fuel prices: the daily national average for regular and diesel, which is where the price box sends you.
