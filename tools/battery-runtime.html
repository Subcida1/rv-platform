RV battery runtime calculator

The math is battery watt-hours divided by the watts you draw. The hard part is knowing the usable part of a battery, which depends on its chemistry, so the tool asks for what is printed on the label and applies the right fraction.

The answer updates as you type. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Your battery bank

How to get it: printed on the battery label as Ah, for ONE battery. Two batteries in parallel add their amp-hours; two in series add volts, not amp-hours. The wiring control below does that arithmetic, so enter one battery's figure and say how they are wired.
Sets how much of the amp-hours you can use. The label says the chemistry: LiFePO4, AGM, or nothing (flooded). Lead-acid chemistries give up about half their amp-hours before the recommended recharge point; lithium gives most of them, and 80 percent is the conservative default.
Parallel batteries add their amp-hours. Batteries in series add volts and keep the amp-hours, so a pair of 225 Ah 6-volt batteries in series is a 225 Ah 12-volt bank, not 450.
Used only by the series option: 24 for two 12-volt batteries in series, 48 for four. Empty means a 12-volt bank.

What you are running

How to get it: add the watts printed on each item's label. The common 12-volt draws: an LED bulb is about 2, the furnace fan 45 to 60 while it runs, the water pump 50 to 110 while it runs, a 12-volt television 35 to 50. A 60-watt load is a few LED lights and the television, which is a normal evening.
Optional. Leave empty to see hours of runtime directly. Fill it in to see how many days the bank carries that schedule.
Tick it when the load is household gear plugged into an inverter rather than 12-volt gear wired to the battery. The inverter keeps about 88 percent of what it draws, so the runtime comes out lower and the result shows both figures.

Why chemistry decides the answer

The amp-hours on the label are the whole tank. How much of the tank you may use depends on the chemistry, because discharging a lead-acid battery deeply shortens its cycle life. Battle Born's comparison gives lead-acid chemistries roughly 50 percent usable capacity and lithium 80 to 100 percent, and Renogy states the same split as a sizing rule: for lead-acid, the standard depth of discharge is 50 percent, and for lithium it is 80 percent. This tool uses 50 percent for flooded and AGM, and 80 percent for lithium, which is the conservative end of the 80-to-100 range Battle Born publishes.

The other half of the math is the watt-hours. A 12-volt lithium battery is 12.8 volts nominal, so 100 amp-hours is 1,280 watt-hours, not 1,200. Dakota Lithium prints exactly that on its 100 amp-hour battery. The tool uses 12.8 volts for lithium and 12.0 for lead-acid, and says so in the result.

Batteries in series add volts and keep their amp-hours, and batteries in parallel add amp-hours and keep their volts. So two 225 amp-hour 6-volt golf-cart batteries in series make a 225 amp-hour 12-volt bank, 2,700 watt-hours, and not the 450 amp-hours that adding the two labels together suggests. The wiring control asks which shape the bank is and does that arithmetic, because a bank sized on the doubled figure plans for twice the runtime the bank has.

What the answer means

The result is hours of runtime, not a promise. Cold weather cuts lead-acid capacity, high discharge rates cut it further, and an inverter running 120-volt gear from the batteries adds its own losses: Victron's datasheets put small 12-volt inverters at 87 to 92 percent at their best, and real-world average efficiency is lower than peak. Ticking the inverter box applies 88 percent, which sits inside that measured band, and the result still reads as optimistic because those figures are peak rather than average. The watts to amps calculator converts any appliance label into the 12-volt draw it becomes through an inverter.

Where to go next

The solar sizing calculator answers the follow-up question: how many panel watts it takes to put back what you use each day. The watts to amps calculator handles the conversion side. When the bank is not the problem and the charging is, the battery not charging guide works through the four sources that charge it, and the converter finder names the replacement when the converter is the failed one. The parts page lists the battery, the monitor and the disconnect as separate parts, with what fails on each.

Sources

  • Battle Born Batteries, AGM vs lithium: the 50 percent usable capacity for lead-acid chemistries and 80 to 100 percent for lithium.
  • Renogy, how to size your battery bank: the 50 percent depth of discharge rule for lead-acid and 80 percent for lithium, and the sizing formula this tool follows.
  • Dakota Lithium, 12V 100Ah battery: the 12.8-volt nominal convention, 100 amp-hours as 1,280 watt-hours.
  • Victron Energy, Phoenix inverter datasheets (PDF): 12-volt inverter peak efficiencies of 87 to 92 percent, the basis for the inverter-loss caution.
  • Ask The RV Engineer, RV appliance amp draw: the 12-volt draws in the watts hint, LED, furnace, pump, television.
