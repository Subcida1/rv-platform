FAQ

The questions everyone asks

What's the 80% rule?

Max towing ratings are measured on ideal roads — flat, no wind, sea level. Real driving has hills, passes, gust fronts, and panic stops. The 80% rule keeps loaded weight at or under 80% of rated capacity so the margin survives reality.

Tongue weight — why does it matter?

Tongue (travel trailer) or pin (fifth wheel) weight transfers into your truck and counts against its payload. Too light and the trailer wanders; too heavy and you exceed your payload. About 10–15% of loaded weight for travel trailers, 20–25% for fifth wheels.

Dry weight vs loaded?

UVW (dry) is the factory-empty weight. Loaded adds water (8.34 lb/gal), propane (4.24 lb/gal), and everything you carry. A "lightweight" trailer gains hundreds of pounds before it ever sees a hitch ball.

Is this a guarantee?

No — nothing here replaces a certified scale and your owner's manuals. This is informational math to catch the obvious mismatch before you buy, pack, or tow. Weigh loaded at a CAT scale whenever the load changes.
